# Ⰰ Glagolitic — The Alphabet That Disappeared

I just built a [Glagolitic translator](https://github.com/belarusian/glagolitic-translator) — a tool that converts Russian or English text into one of the oldest Slavic alphabets.

Here's why it's fascinating:

**Glagolitic was invented in 863 AD** by Saints Cyril and Methodius — 300 years before the Renaissance, 600 years before Gutenberg. It was the first alphabet designed specifically for Slavic languages. Every sound had its own letter. Nothing was borrowed from Greek or Latin — the letters were completely original, ornamental, almost mystical in appearance.

Ⰰ Ⰲ Ⰳ Ⰵ Ⰷ Ⰹ Ⰻ Ⰽ Ⰿ Ⱁ Ⱃ Ⱅ Ⱇ Ⱉ Ⱋ

It spread across Great Moravia, Bulgaria, Serbia, and Croatia. The most famous surviving artifact is the **Baška Tablet** (1100 AD) — a stone inscription now displayed in the Croatian Parliament.

**But then it vanished.**

Not because it was bad. In fact, it was *better* than its successor — more phonetically precise, more expressive. It disappeared because:

1. **Cyrillic was easier** — based on Greek, familiar to scholars
2. **Politics** — rulers promoted Cyrillic as the "official" script
3. **Printing** — Glagolitic's ornamental curves were a nightmare for typesetters
4. **Modernity** — by the 19th century, even its last stronghold in Croatia gave up

The last regular Glagolitic mass was celebrated in the 1970s.

It's a reminder that the tools we use today — our alphabet, our conventions — aren't inevitable. They're the result of power, convenience, and historical accident. A beautiful script was erased not by evolution, but by politics.

I built this translator as a small act of digital preservation. Open the HTML file, type in Russian or English, and watch your words transform into a script that the world forgot.
